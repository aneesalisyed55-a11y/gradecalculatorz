What Is a Semester Grade Calculator?

A semester grade calculator combines the grades that contribute to a semester result. Depending on your school, those components may include:

  • First and second quarter grades
  • Midterm or semester examination
  • Coursework and assignments
  • Projects, quizzes, and tests
  • Separate course grades and their credit values

A semester grade is not automatically the same as a GPA. A semester grade usually reports performance for a grading period or course, while GPA converts course grades into grade points. Use the GPA Calculator if you want to calculate grade point average from course grades and credits.

Schools do not all use the same semester structure. Some combine two quarters and an exam, while others use grading periods without a semester exam. The correct calculation depends on your school's report card rules, syllabus, or academic handbook.

How to Calculate a Semester Grade

First, identify the components that make up your semester grade. Then find the weight assigned to each component.

When the weights are percentages that total 100%, use:

Semester Grade = Sum of (Component Grade × Component Weight) ÷ 100

What Grade Do You Need on Your Semester Exam?

If your grading periods are complete and only the semester exam remains, you can calculate the score needed to reach a target semester grade.

When the completed periods have a combined weight of 1 − exam weight, use:

Required Exam Score = (Target Semester Grade − Completed Contribution) ÷ Exam Weight

Example:

  • Average of completed grading periods: 90%
  • Desired semester grade: 92%
  • Semester exam weight: 20%
Required exam score = (92 − 90 × 0.80) ÷ 0.20
= (92 − 72) ÷ 0.20
= 20 ÷ 0.20
= 100% — you need 100% on the exam to finish with 92%

This assumes the completed-period average represents the other 80% of the semester. For a course with one current grade and one final exam rather than several grading periods, use the Final Grade Calculator. You can also try the "Exam score I need" mode in the calculator at the top of this page.

Semester Grade vs. Cumulative GPA

A semester grade describes performance within a particular semester or grading period. A cumulative GPA combines grade points from multiple courses and terms according to the institution's rules.

For a GPA calculation, each course's grade-point value is commonly multiplied by its credit hours. The quality points are then divided by the total graded credits included in the calculation. The exact treatment of withdrawals, pass/fail courses, repeats, and incomplete grades varies by institution.

Use the GPA Calculator to calculate a GPA from course grades and credit hours.

What If Your School Uses Letter Grades?

If your report card shows letters rather than percentages, do not assume that every letter has one universal numerical equivalent.

A B may represent a different percentage range at different schools. Check the grading scale supplied by your school. If the institution provides a grade-point or percentage conversion table, use that table consistently for all periods.

When only a letter is available and no conversion is published, a precise percentage cannot be derived from the letter alone.

Semester Grades in High School and College

High School Semester Grades

High schools may calculate semester grades from quarters, six-week periods, midterms, final exams, or a combination of these. Districts may publish a common policy, while some course-level rules may differ.

Check the report card key, student handbook, and course syllabus to find the components and weights.

College and University Semester Grades

College courses more commonly use a course syllabus that assigns weights to coursework, quizzes, labs, projects, midterms, and final exams. A college semester GPA is a separate calculation that uses course grade points and credits.

If you need the grade for one course before the final, use the Grade Calculator or Final Grade Calculator, depending on whether you are calculating the current result or the required exam score.

Common Semester Grade Calculation Mistakes

  • Assuming every school uses 40/40/20.
  • Treating all grading periods as equally weighted without checking.
  • Forgetting a midterm or semester exam.
  • Entering a course's latest test score instead of its grading-period average.
  • Converting letter grades using a scale that does not belong to your school.
  • Confusing a semester percentage with a semester GPA.
  • Rounding each component before adding the weighted contributions.

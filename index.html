What Is a Grade Calculator?

A grade calculator is a tool that works out an academic score from assessment results. Depending on the grading method, it can calculate a percentage, an average, or a weighted course grade.

A single test score is usually calculated from points earned and points possible. A course grade may require combining several assessments, and a weighted course grade gives each assessment the influence specified by the syllabus.

How to Calculate Your Grade

The correct formula depends on how your teacher or institution scores the work. These are the most common methods.

1. Calculate a Grade From Points

When an assessment is marked by points, divide the points earned by the total points possible and multiply by 100.

Formula:

Percentage = (Points Earned / Points Possible) × 100

Example: You earn 42 points out of 50.

(42 / 50) × 100 = 84%

Your score is 84%. The letter grade associated with 84% depends on the grading scale your school uses.

2. Calculate an Unweighted Grade Average

If every assignment has equal importance, add the scores and divide by the number of scores.

Formula:

Average = Sum of scores / Number of scores

For example, your scores are 85%, 90%, 78%, and 92%.

(85 + 90 + 78 + 92) / 4 = 86.25%

Your unweighted average is 86.25%. This method is not appropriate when a final exam or another assessment has a different weight from the rest of your work.

3. Calculate a Weighted Grade

A weighted grade is used when assignments or categories contribute different percentages to the course result.

Formula when weights total 100%:

Weighted Grade = Sum of (Score × Weight) / 100

Current Grade vs. Final Course Grade

Your current grade is based on the assessments included in the calculation at the time you check it. Your final course grade is the result after all required assessments and course rules have been applied.

A learning management system may show a running grade that excludes ungraded assignments, includes them as zeros, or calculates categories in a particular way. Check the gradebook settings and syllabus before comparing its displayed result with a manual calculation.

If you already know your current grade and want to estimate the exam score required to reach a target, the Final Grade Calculator is the more suitable tool.

Understanding Letter Grades and Percentage Scales

A percentage is a numerical result. A letter grade is assigned by applying an institution's grading scale to that result. There is no single grading scale used by every school, college, or country.

The following is an illustrative US-style scale, not a universal standard:

Letter gradeExample percentage range
A90-100%
B80-89%
C70-79%
D60-69%
FBelow 60%

Some institutions divide these ranges into plus and minus grades, and some use different cutoffs. For example, Arizona State University publishes its own grade-point values and rules for GPA calculations. Always use the scale in your course syllabus or registrar's policy.

Grading in the United Kingdom

UK undergraduate honours degrees commonly use degree classifications such as First-Class Honours, Upper Second-Class Honours (2:1), Lower Second-Class Honours (2:2), and Third-Class Honours. The exact regulations and borderline rules are set by each university. Do not apply a US A-F chart to a UK university result without checking the institution's rules.

Grading in Canada

Canadian institutions use different combinations of percentages, letter grades, and grade points. The University of Toronto's Scarborough campus, for example, publishes its own percentage and grade-point ranges. Other Canadian universities may use different scales.

Grading in Australia

Australian universities commonly use categories such as High Distinction, Distinction, Credit, Pass, and Fail, but boundaries vary by institution. The Australian National University publishes its own grading scale. Check your university's assessment policy before converting a percentage into a grade category.

Tips for Accurate Grade Calculations

  • Read the syllabus to confirm each assessment's weight.
  • Use the actual score and maximum points for each assessment.
  • Check whether the gradebook excludes ungraded work or treats it as zero.
  • Do not use a simple average when assessments have different weights.
  • Keep full precision during the calculation and round only the final result as required by your institution.
  • Confirm the official letter-grade boundaries and pass requirements with your school.
  • Check for special rules such as dropped scores, extra credit, retakes, or a minimum final-exam score.

What Is Grade Point Average (GPA)?

Grade Point Average, commonly called GPA, is a numerical summary of academic performance. In many US colleges, each letter grade is assigned a numerical grade-point value. The value is multiplied by the course's credit hours to calculate quality points.

The quality points from included courses are added together and divided by the total credits included in the GPA calculation.

A GPA is not simply the average of course percentages. It depends on the institution's letter-grade conversion and the credit hours assigned to each course.

GPA systems differ between schools, colleges, and countries. This page explains the common credit-weighted calculation used by many US institutions. Always check your registrar's grading policy for the official grade-point values and rules.

How to Calculate GPA

The common credit-weighted formula is:

GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total GPA Credits

For each course:

Course Quality Points = Grade Point Value × Course Credits

Weighted GPA vs. Unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA uses the standard grade-point values assigned by the school without adding extra points for course difficulty.

A weighted GPA may give additional grade points for approved advanced courses, such as honors, Advanced Placement, International Baccalaureate, or dual-enrollment classes. The extra weighting is determined by the school or district.

Semester GPA vs. Cumulative GPA

Semester GPA

Semester GPA measures the grade-point average for courses included in one semester. It is calculated using the grade points and credits from that semester.

Cumulative GPA

Cumulative GPA combines eligible coursework across multiple terms. It is not usually calculated by taking a simple average of semester GPA figures when the semesters contain different numbers of credits.

A useful formula is:

Cumulative GPA = Total Quality Points Across Terms ÷ Total Included Credits Across Terms

Example:

  • Previous GPA: 3.20 across 30 credits
  • New semester GPA: 3.60 across 15 credits
Previous quality points: 3.20 × 30 = 96
New semester quality points: 3.60 × 15 = 54
Combined GPA: (96 + 54) ÷ (30 + 15) = 150 ÷ 45
= 3.33 — the cumulative GPA, approximately

You can try this with your own numbers in the "Cumulative GPA" mode of the calculator at the top of this page.

Credit hours determine how strongly each course affects your GPA. A four-credit course has twice the credit weight of a two-credit course.

Can You Convert a Percentage Directly to GPA?

There is no single official percentage-to-GPA conversion that applies to every institution. A percentage is first interpreted using the school's grading scale. The corresponding letter grade is then assigned its grade-point value under that institution's rules.

For example, the same percentage may map to different letter grades at different universities. A calculator that converts every percentage directly to one universal GPA value can therefore produce a result that does not match your transcript.

Use your institution's official grading chart. If you need to calculate your course percentage before converting it, use the Grade Calculator.

GPA in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia

United States

Many US colleges use a 4.0 GPA scale, but grade-point values, plus/minus rules, repeat policies, and exclusions vary. Some high schools also calculate weighted GPA for advanced courses.

United Kingdom

UK universities commonly report undergraduate achievement through degree classifications rather than a US-style 4.0 GPA. A direct conversion should not be assumed. Use the university's official transcript or conversion guidance when an application requests a GPA equivalent.

Canada

Canadian universities use institution-specific grading systems. Some report letter grades and grade points, while others emphasize percentages or different GPA scales. Use the conversion rules published by the university.

Australia

Australian universities commonly use grades such as High Distinction, Distinction, Credit, Pass, and Fail. These results should not be converted to a US 4.0 GPA using a generic chart unless the receiving institution provides or accepts that conversion.

What Can Change an Official GPA?

A calculator can estimate GPA from the grades and credits entered, but the official transcript may apply additional rules.

  • Pass/fail courses: Some pass grades do not contribute to GPA, while failing grades may be treated differently.
  • Withdrawals: A withdrawal may be excluded, but a withdrawal-failing grade may count.
  • Incomplete grades: Treatment depends on institutional policy and whether the grade is later completed.
  • Repeated courses: Some institutions replace an earlier grade; others include both attempts or apply limits.
  • Transfer credits: Transfer courses may count toward degree credit without being included in the institution's GPA.
  • Advanced-course weighting: High schools may add points for approved advanced courses.
  • Rounding: Institutions determine how many decimal places appear on official records.

For example, the University of Massachusetts Amherst explains which grade symbols are included in its GPA calculation, while Arizona State University publishes its own grade-point values and calculation method. These policies are institution-specific.

How to Improve Your GPA

If you want to understand how future grades may affect your GPA, start by checking your current GPA and the credits it represents.

You can estimate a future cumulative GPA by combining your existing quality points with the quality points you expect to earn in upcoming courses. Remember that a large number of completed credits makes the cumulative result less responsive to one new term.

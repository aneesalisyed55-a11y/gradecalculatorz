What Is a Final Grade Calculator?

A final grade calculator works backward from a target course result. It estimates how well you need to perform on the remaining final exam to finish the course with a particular percentage.

For example, you may currently have 84% in a class and want to finish with 90%. If the final exam is worth 25% of the course grade, the calculator uses the remaining 75% and the exam's 25% contribution to find the score required.

This is different from a general Grade Calculator, which combines the scores you have already received to calculate your current grade.

You can also use it after the exam to estimate your final course grade. The result depends on the accuracy of your current grade and exam weight, so check your syllabus or course gradebook before relying on it.

How to Use the Final Grade Calculator

1

Enter Your Current Grade

Enter your current course percentage before the final exam. Use the overall course grade, not just your latest test score.

If your learning platform displays a running grade, check whether it includes ungraded assignments and whether category weights are applied.

2

Enter Your Desired Final Grade

Enter the course percentage you want to achieve. This could be your target for an A, B, passing grade, or another result required by your program.

Do not assume that a particular percentage always represents the same letter grade. Use your school's grading scale.

3

Enter the Final Exam Weight

Find the final exam's percentage weight in your course syllabus or official grading breakdown. For example, enter 20 if the final contributes 20% of the overall course grade.

The exam weight is not the percentage of questions on the exam. It is the share of the course grade determined by the exam.

4

Calculate the Required Score

Calculate the result and check whether it is within the possible exam score range. A required score above 100% means the target cannot be reached through the final exam alone under the entered assumptions.

Final Grade Formula

When the current grade represents all coursework before the final and the final exam is the only remaining component, use:

Required Final Exam Score = (Target Grade − Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight)) ÷ Final Weight

Enter the final weight as a decimal. For example, 20% becomes 0.20.

The formula comes from the weighted course-grade equation:

Final Course Grade = Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight) + Final Exam Score × Final Weight

Worked Example: What Do I Need on a Final Worth 20%?

Suppose:

  • Current grade: 88%
  • Desired course grade: 90%
  • Final exam weight: 20%

Convert the exam weight to 0.20.

Required score = (90 − 88 × 0.80) ÷ 0.20
= (90 − 70.4) ÷ 0.20
= 19.6 ÷ 0.20
= 98% — you need 98% on the final exam to finish with 90%

If you score 100%, your final course grade would be:

88 × 0.80 + 100 × 0.20→70.4 + 20
= 90.4%

These results assume the current grade and weight are correct and there are no additional course rules.

The Best and Worst Cases

What Is the Highest Grade You Can Get on the Final?

Your maximum possible course grade is found by assuming you score 100% on the final:

Maximum Course Grade = Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight) + 100 × Final Weight

For example, if your current grade is 82% and the final is worth 30%:

82 × 0.70 + 100 × 0.30→57.4 + 30
= 87.4% — your highest possible course grade

This helps you set a realistic target before the exam. It does not account for extra credit, dropped assignments, retakes, grade curves, or other policies unless those are already reflected in your current grade.

What Happens If You Get Zero on the Final?

The lowest course grade under the simple formula is the current grade multiplied by the portion of the course already completed:

Minimum Course Grade = Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight)

For a current grade of 82% and a final worth 30%:

82 × 0.70→57.4
= 57.4% with a zero on the final

Some courses have a separate rule requiring students to sit or pass the final exam, so check the syllabus.

Calculate Your Final Course Grade After the Exam

If you already know your final exam score, use the weighted course-grade formula:

Final Course Grade = Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight) + Exam Score × Final Weight

Example:

  • Grade before final: 86%
  • Final exam score: 90%
  • Final exam weight: 20%
86 × 0.80 + 90 × 0.20→68.8 + 18
= 86.8% — your calculated course grade

For a course with several remaining assignments rather than one final exam, use the main Grade Calculator to combine all assessment categories and weights. You can also try the "Grade after exam" mode in the calculator at the top of this page.

What If the Course Uses Points Instead of Percentages?

Some instructors assign a fixed number of points to each assessment. In that case, the final's weight can be calculated from the total course points.

Suppose you have earned 530 points out of 600 possible points, and the final exam is worth 200 points. The course will have 800 points in total if no other points are added.

The final represents 25% of the total course points:

200 ÷ 800→0.25 (25%)

If you want 90% overall, you need 720 total points. You already have 530, so you need 190 points on the final:

720 − 530→190 points
190 ÷ 200 × 100→95%
You need 95% on the final exam

Final Exam vs. Semester Grade Calculation

A final grade calculator usually combines a current course grade with one remaining exam. A semester grade calculator may combine several grading periods, such as two quarters and a semester exam.

If your school uses separate quarter or term marks, use the Semester Grade Calculator. If you need to calculate the current grade from assignments, use the Grade Calculator.

After a course is complete, you can use the GPA Calculator to calculate grade points across courses and credit hours.

Common Mistakes When Calculating a Final Grade

  • Using a test score as the current course grade: Enter the overall current course percentage, not one assessment result.
  • Guessing the final weight: Check the syllabus or official course outline.
  • Mixing percentages and whole numbers: Use 20% as 0.20 in the formula, not 20.
  • Ignoring remaining coursework: If other assignments remain, the simple one-exam formula is incomplete.
  • Assuming the target is possible: Check the maximum possible course grade.
  • Assuming a passing threshold: Some programs require a minimum grade in a specific course or a minimum score on the final itself.
  • Rounding too early: Keep decimals during the calculation and apply the institution's rounding policy to the final result.
